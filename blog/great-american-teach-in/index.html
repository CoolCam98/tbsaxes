Saxophones in the classroom at the Great American Teach-In

Last week we packed up our horns and spent the day at two Hillsborough County public schools, including Summerfield Crossings Elementary, for the Great American Teach-In. We played for class after class, talked with students about what a life in music looks like, and fielded a lot of great questions about the saxophone.

What Is the Great American Teach-In?

The Great American Teach-In is a yearly day when Hillsborough County Public Schools opens its classrooms to the community. Parents, business owners, first responders, artists, and other professionals visit schools across the district to talk with students about their careers and hobbies, lead an activity, or teach a short lesson. More than 230 schools in the district take part, and Pinellas and Pasco county schools hold their own Teach-Ins each November as well. This year's Hillsborough Teach-In was on Thursday, November 19.

The idea is simple and a lot of fun: students get to hear from real people doing real jobs, and see a few more paths they might take after graduation.

A Live Set in Every Classroom

Every visit started with music. We played a short set pulled from our regular jazz, funk, and pop repertoire, so students could hear what five saxophones and a drummer sound like up close. Live music hits differently when it's ten feet away, and it was a blast watching a room full of students light up when the horns came in.

Meet the Saxophone Family

After the music, we talked about the instrument itself. The saxophone was invented by Adolphe Sax, a Belgian instrument maker, in the 1840s. Even though it's made of brass, it's part of the woodwind family because the sound starts with a reed vibrating against the mouthpiece. Our lineup covers four members of the saxophone family: soprano, alto, tenor, and baritone. Hearing them side by side, from the bright, high soprano down to the deep bari, is one of the best ways to understand how a section fits together.

Careers in Music, and Why Practice Matters

We also talked about careers in music. Some musicians perform full time, many teach, and others work in recording, composing, music production, or the business side of the industry. Plenty of great players build music into their lives alongside a different career, which is how a lot of community bands and groups like ours come together.

The message we kept coming back to was practice. Every musician in the Collective got here the same way, with a little practice most days for a long time. Fifteen focused minutes every day will take a young player further than a long session once a week, and that habit carries over into just about everything else in school and in life.

Where Music Can Take You

Our favorite part was talking about the places music can take you. School band opens the door to trips, competitions, and performances in front of big crowds, and those experiences keep going long after graduation. For us, music has meant festival stages, weddings, holiday events, and some of the best friendships we have. We hope a few students left thinking about picking up an instrument, or sticking with the one they already play.

Thank you to the teachers and staff who welcomed us, and to every student who asked a question. Special thanks to Summerfield Crossings Elementary for the certificate of appreciation. You made it a great day.
