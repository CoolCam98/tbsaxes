Weddings

Live Saxophone for Your Wedding Day.

A wedding day has more moving parts musically than almost any other event — and a saxophone group is one of the few live-music options flexible enough to cover every part of it without a full sound crew or a seven-piece band taking over your venue. Here's how we typically structure a wedding, and what it's like working with us from ceremony to last dance.

Ceremony

For the ceremony itself, we keep it simple and elegant — a soft instrumental prelude while guests are seated, a processional that actually sounds like a processional, and a recessional with a little more lift to send everyone into cocktail hour. One or two saxophones is usually plenty here; you don't need six horns for a twenty-minute ceremony, and we'll tell you that upfront instead of upselling you into more than the moment calls for.

Cocktail Hour

This is where the saxophone-group format really shines. Jazz standards, easy funk, a little bossa nova — music that fills the room and gives your guests something to talk about, without competing with conversation. We read the room and adjust in real time: if people are gathered around and chatting, we keep it warm and unobtrusive. If the energy's already building, we lean into it.

Reception

Once dinner service starts, we usually dial things back down — background music that supports conversation without drowning it out. Then, as the night moves toward dancing, we bring the full group back in and build toward a set that actually gets people on the floor. Funk, soul, Motown, and enough current pop covers — on horns — that nobody's checking their phone.

What a Typical Wedding Set Looks Like

Every wedding is different, but a common structure looks something like this: prelude, processional, and recessional during the ceremony; roughly 60–90 minutes of cocktail-hour music; a lower-key background set through dinner; and two to three high-energy sets during the reception itself, with breaks worked in around toasts, cake, and the first dance. We build the exact timeline around your day-of schedule — just send it over.

Song List & Genres

We don't hand you a fixed setlist and ask you to pick from it — we build a set around what you and your partner actually like. That said, our bread and butter is jazz standards, Motown and soul classics, funk, R&B, and a rotating list of current pop songs that happen to sound great on saxophones. If there's a song you need for a first dance or a parent dance, tell us and we'll learn it.

Booking Your Wedding Date

Every quote is built around your group size, set length, and location, so we don't post a flat rate here — reach out with your date and we'll get you real numbers within a day or two. Peak wedding season in the Tampa Bay area (May through October, especially Friday and Saturday dates) books up early, so the sooner you reach out, the more flexibility you'll have.
